## Intent
Current menus and HUD are functional interim UI, not approved final visual quality. The final direction requires dedicated illustrated equipment/upgrade screens and a genuine navigable skill-tree screen with connected nodes, branches, prerequisites and clear locked/available/unlocked states. A text-based upgrade list does not fulfill this requirement. Tree mechanics and final UI art direction still need a separately scoped design/implementation phase; Phase 4 is not that phase.
Create a coherent, readable laboratory excavation experience with satisfying physical tools and recognizable egg/creature themes. Preserve useful existing art while reviewing actual in-game appearance. Interfaces should feel integrated into a Roblox game. Avoid accumulated decoration that obscures interaction.
The laboratory is an imaginative discovery workshop: polished, playful, expressive cartoon excavation. Use bold silhouettes, slightly exaggerated proportions, clean bevels, readable mechanical construction and intentional material/color hierarchy. Industrial detail supports this identity rather than a cold military or realistic mood. The starter should be well designed while leaving room for impressive future tiers.
Avoid generic primitive assemblies as finished assets, flat-recolor upgrades, random noise/scratches/grime, uniformly dark presentation and excessive glow or particles concealing weak modeling. Detail must remain useful at gameplay distance.
Resolve flicker, overlapping surfaces, clipping, awkward handling, confusing feedback and scale inconsistencies as visible defects. Neither absence of errors nor a historical completion report establishes quality.

## Reference and approval process
1. Agree on visual reference and design intent before production.
2. Establish silhouette, proportions and scale against the player and representative shell section.
3. Develop purposeful geometry and mechanical detail.
4. Prepare UVs and appropriate supported material maps.
5. Deliver previews from useful angles, including held-tool/grip context.
6. Import and integrate into Roblox within the authorized phase.
7. Deliver for the user's manual in-game review and resolve feedback before expansion.

## Motion, effects and sound direction
Connect anticipation, acceleration, contact, follow-through, recoil and recovery; imply weight without locking movement unnecessarily. Ordinary hits, precise/stronger hits, progressive cracking, final break, collection and upgrade reception need distinct restrained signals.
Local chips, brief contact accents and readable collection arcs should explain the result without concealing the target. Plan for concurrent players, mobile visibility, reduced motion and reduced effects. Sound should combine an airy preparation, short metal/ceramic contact and dry mineral fracture, aligned to contact; sound assets are not produced in Phase 1A. Performance budgets are targets, not measurements.

## Asset requirements
Tools require correct grips, pivots, moving parts and animation requirements; design heavy drills for two hands from the outset.
Materials must communicate intentional surfaces through suitable color, roughness, normal, metalness and other supported maps as needed. Arbitrary cracks, random noise, stretched UVs and flat recolors do not meet the bar.
Egg themes must connect silhouette, shell, cracking, debris, materials, audio, reveal, gameplay and creature identity.
Balance geometry and texture sizes with actual gameplay visibility and mobile performance. No performance claims without evidence.

## Preservation and review package
Keep editable Blender files, texture sources, exports and metadata together under existing src/models conventions. Record scale/orientation, grip/pivot locations, moving parts, texture/map roles, export settings, uploaded asset IDs, owner and permission dependencies.
Preserve existing assets before replacements. Explain large-file storage limitations rather than silently omitting sources.
